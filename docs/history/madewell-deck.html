An ontology of work

Made Well

Work has four acts. They recur at every scale.
Everything else is scaffolding.

The problem

Sixteen words for four positions.

poolqueuevalvestagephasecycle outerinnerdiscoverycommitbuildland rumencartridgestriationwall

Six metaphor systems — plumbing, gaming, biology, geology, computing, construction — describing a process with four positions. Every one of them had to be memorised, and none of them taught you anything you didn't already know.

The invariant

Think it through. Decide the next cut.
Make it. Check it.

done when
ideate
understand what you're making, and why
you can say it in a sentence — and say what's not in it
plan
decide the next actionable cut
there is a next cut, and you know what it waits on
implement
make the thing
it exists and the happy path works
verify
check it became what you imagined
someone who didn't make it says so
No metaphor lives here. These are plain verbs, which is exactly why this part never confused anyone — and everything else did.
Depth

A chair needs legs. A leg needs a tenon.

the chair  ideate → plan → implement → verify
└ a leg  ideate → plan → implement → verify
└ its tenon  ideate → plan → implement → verify

The outer pass is the whole piece. The inner pass is one component. Same four acts, one depth down — as deep as the work goes and no deeper.

There is no second vocabulary for the outer pass. discovery · commit · build · land — those were the four acts at the top, wearing different clothes. Four words, not eight.
The kernel

Two practices, one failure —
and it isn't getting things wrong.

It's re-deciding something that was already decided.

Tests cannot see this failure. Code that reinvents a design system you already built is correct, well-written, and passes everything. The defect isn't in the work — it's in what the work didn't know.

Practice one · covers ideate + plan

Ground

In etching, the ground is the acid-resist laid on the plate before you cut a single line. No mark before the ground is laid.

Here: make the existing decisions present before you design anything. What is already built, already named, already ruled on.

The dominant failure in this work is not choosing wrong. It is never thinking to look.
Practice two · covers implement + verify

The Jig

A jig makes the wrong cut physically impossible. Not advice. Not a warning. A stop.

A test asks

Does it work?

A jig asks

Did you re-decide something?

Perfect CSS that reinvents your design system passes every test and is still wrong. No correctness gate can see that. A jig can.

Every shop fills up with them, and each one is a fossilised correction. Corrections accumulate; the recurring expensive ones become stops; stops that stop earning their place come off the wall.

A stop starts as a warning and graduates to a block once the existing violations are cleared.

Material

Three states, and no others.

state
what it means
stock
what you have, uncut. Grows without limit — that is correct, not a backlog.
on the bench
what you are working. One pair of hands per piece.
finished
proofed, and off the bench.
There is no admission gate. You take stock to the bench. Nothing is rejected — it is just still on the rack.
The shop

Four hands minimum.
Six at most.

Four, because the acts must not collapse into each other. Whoever imagined it should not be the one who proves it worked. Whoever cut it should not be the one who signs it off.

You don't proof your own plate.

One pair of hands doing all four grades its own work — and when that fails, the failure gets blamed on the material. Six at most, because past that the coordination costs more than the work.

Why plans bend

You have a destination.
You do not have a route.

You plan the cut. Then the material tells you what it will and won't do, and you adjust. The next cut is chosen by whoever is steering, whatever is most urgent, and what the material turns out to be.

That is not a planning failure. It is the condition.

So this is built for cheap correction, not plan adherence — short cuts, small pieces, nothing half-made for six weeks. You can read grain on a short cut and recover. You cannot on a long one.

Composition

A trade is a set of grounds and jigs
for a kind of work.

The shop is the same whether you are a joiner or a bookbinder. What differs is the trade. You load the one for the work in front of you.

May add

an operation at any edge — before ideate, between plan and implement, after verify.

May never replace

one of the four. Adding is composition. Replacing is a different framework wearing this one's name.

How decisions get made

Three questions, asked at
every fork.

#
does this…
1
lead toward something ten times better to use? Not a bit better — marginal rarely repays its complication, and that cost lands on whoever comes next.
2
point toward something anyone would remember, or love? Forgettable is a failure state. Correct and joyless is still a miss.
3
get closer to being as easy for an agent as for a person? Two users, always.
Directions, not measurements. You cannot score these, and an invented number is worse than an honest judgement — but you can always say which of two options points further.
Forbidden

Three rules that do not flex.

rule
why
no rewind
Work moves one direction. A failed proof doesn't send the piece backward — it stays on the bench and you cut again. Attempts accumulate; position doesn't move until it passes.
no self-proofing
Restated, because it is the one people skip.
no silent pass
If a stop is missing, say the stop is missing. A rule with no jig behind it is a preference, and should be labelled one.
One pass, end to end

A settings page

Ordinary work. Nothing clever. Watch where each practice earns its place.

act
what happens
ideate
Making: a page where someone changes their email. Not making: account deletion. Done when: the change persists and survives a reload.
plan
Ground first: the repo already has a form primitive, a token set, and a mutation helper. Don't design a form — compose one. Three files, not eleven.
implement
One component built from what exists. No new CSS.
verify
A second pair of hands runs it. The jig confirms zero raw hex — proving the token set was actually used, which no test would have caught.
Coda

Four verbs, two practices,
three states, one shop.

Everything that was confusing turned out to be scaffolding — and scaffolding can come down without touching the building.
