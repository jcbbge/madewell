# Foundation — Positioning

**Decided before anything is made. Everything downstream inherits it or contradicts it.**

---

## Principles

1. **Positioning is a choice to exclude.** If it does not rule anyone out, it is not
   positioning, it is a description.
2. **It names an alternative.** People do not buy in a vacuum; they switch, or they do
   nothing. Nothing is the most common competitor and the one most often omitted.
3. **It is written down, in one place, in one paragraph.** Positioning held in several heads
   is several positionings.
4. **It changes rarely and deliberately.** Re-deciding it mid-campaign is the marketing
   equivalent of re-deciding the schema mid-build: everything already made becomes wrong.

## Protocol

1. **Name the person.** A specific one. A role, a situation, a moment — not a demographic.
2. **Name the alternative.** What they do today. Include *nothing* as a candidate and take it
   seriously.
3. **Name the switch cost.** What it costs them to change — money, time, risk, dignity.
4. **Name the wedge.** The one thing true of us and not of the alternative, that they care
   about. One. A list of five means it has not been found yet.
5. **Name the anti-audience.** Who this is explicitly not for. If this is hard, return to 1.
6. **Write the paragraph.** For *(person)* who *(situation)*, we are *(category)* that
   *(wedge)*, unlike *(alternative)*, because *(proof)*.
7. **Test it against a real conversation.** Not a survey — a conversation. If it does not
   survive one, it will not survive a market.

## Definition of done

- One paragraph exists, in one file, that a stranger could read and correctly say who this is
  *not* for.
- The alternative is named, and *doing nothing* was considered.
- The wedge is one thing, and there is a source behind it.
- At least one real person outside the team has heard it and reacted.
