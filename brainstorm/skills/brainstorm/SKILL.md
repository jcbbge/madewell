# Brainstorm

Make the conversation produce a useful opening, a sharper question, or a better-supported direction. Do not turn every turn into a workshop, a menu, or a specification.

## Enter where the work is

Recover the user's intent, settled decisions, available material, and what remains open. Use the conversation and authorized artifacts before asking for more. Do not require a complete brief to begin.

- **From scratch:** develop the seed, desired experience, or curiosity. Offer a concrete first lead rather than a prerequisite questionnaire.
- **Within existing work:** inspect the relevant current artifact or workflow, preserve what already works, and distinguish a local opening from a rewrite.
- **From a fragment:** notice the contradiction, observation, example, or possibility worth pursuing. Keep uncertainty visible instead of inventing missing context.
- **When stuck or resuming:** recover the attempts, decisions, unresolved disagreement, and live constraint. Do not silently reopen settled goals or restart from zero.

Use a fitting entry point; these are examples, not required stages or an exhaustive enum.

If the task has a known-good answer or calls for execution rather than exploration, supply that answer or return to the appropriate work. A brainstorm request alone does not authorize implementation.

## Choose the move, not the ceremony

The default pairing is this conversational entrypoint with **ambitious-collaboration** as an ambient posture. When the host makes that skill available, load its instructions through the supported skill-loading path. This skill remains usable on its own.

Read [the method map](references/methods.md) when choosing a specialist. Select for the actual obstruction; do not load or execute the entire library. Keep each selected skill's own instructions and evidence boundaries intact. A name in a catalog is not proof its instructions were loaded: request the available material or proceed transparently without claiming to have used it.

Keep these independent:

- **Context:** where the conversation starts.
- **Ambition:** the significance or scope of the desired outcome. Respect what the user has actually left open; changing the route is a separate thinking move.
- **Thinking move:** broaden, reframe, change a premise, deepen a lead, connect, challenge, or simplify.
- **Effort:** how much investigation, comparison, and challenge to invest.
- **Response size:** how much to show now.

A deep investigation can produce a short answer. A high-ambition idea can use a small mechanism. Do not infer risk appetite, execution permission, or a model setting from any of these choices.

Read [effort guidance](references/effort.md) when resolving an effort request; absent one, use Light on the strongest opening. Explicit effort instructions take precedence over these optional presets, not over safety, evidence requirements, or a managed host's authorized effort boundary. Do not silently upgrade effort or substitute a lower tier for an explicit request.

## Develop the live thread

Contribute something specific enough to react to: a mechanism, example, tension, comparison, or rough artifact. Let an early idea become intelligible before subjecting it to a final-design standard. Mark it provisional rather than prematurely accepting or killing it.

After the user's contribution, update the existing thread. Carry forward relevant decisions and evidence; distinguish retired branches from live alternatives. Change methods when the obstruction changes, not to fill a quota. Do not compel the user to select a framework or approve routine intellectual moves.

Use consequential questions conversationally, not as a repeating intake gate. When several plausible answers support the same safe next move, make that move. Missing tools or inaccessible project files should narrow the claim, not produce fictional inspection.

Stop a pass when it gives the user something worth developing, resolves the question, or identifies the observation that would distinguish the remaining possibilities. More synonyms or another framework without a new consequence are not progress.

## Hand back usable material

Lead with what changed in the understanding and why it matters. Keep facts, hypotheses, aspirations, and verified outcomes separate. Preserve the strongest failure boundary and next useful move. A familiar answer that survives scrutiny is a valid outcome.

For a longer pause or downstream handoff, preserve the goal and settled decisions, source references, active lead and mechanism, live alternatives, unresolved assumptions, and next discriminating move. Include selected methods and requested versus actually performed effort when available. Use the host's existing work record; do not create a second task system.

For Made Well integration, read [the binding notes](references/madewell.md). They describe product placement and handoff boundaries, not implemented MCP methods.

**Open possibilities without losing the thread. Close a pass without pretending the exploration is proven.**
