# Synthesizing Insights

A synthesis earns its place by explaining a relationship or changing a decision that separate summaries did not. A tidy framework is not itself evidence of a real pattern.

## Preserve the source before abstracting it

For each consequential claim, retain its source, subject, relevant conditions, time horizon, and whether it is observation, interpretation, or proposal. Follow quotations and summaries to their common origin where available. Several retellings of one result are not several independent observations.

Do not let compression silently change "some users in this setting" into "users," association into causation, or a possible explanation into an established result. Missing evidence remains missing even when the narrative becomes coherent.

## Align relationships, not familiar words

Identify the proposed relation: causal, structural, functional, temporal, or merely analogous. Map the entities and the operation that connects them. Similar nouns, shapes, or vocabulary do not establish the same mechanism.

For a transfer, name the shared constraint and the material difference that could break it. Fixed capacity and replaceable capacity may share a booking interface while requiring different decisions. Preserve that difference rather than forcing the examples into one abstraction.

Move up to a general statement, then back down to each concrete case. If the statement only fits after changing a case's facts, narrow it or reject it. Do not force every input into a universal principle.

## Treat disagreement as information

Before resolving an apparent contradiction, compare definitions, populations, baselines, conditions, and time horizons. Two claims may be compatible under different conditions; if so, state the conditional relationship.

If equally relevant evidence still disagrees, preserve the live alternatives and identify what would distinguish them. Do not average away a consequential difference, choose the more fluent account, or solicit the user's agreement as a substitute for factual verification.

## Derive something that can fail

State what follows from the proposed connection that was not merely copied from the inputs: a boundary, a prediction, a new option, or a different intervention. Identify a counterexample or observation that would weaken it. A pattern generated from examples has not been independently validated by restating those same examples.

Check an unused case or source when available and proportionate. If none is available, label the synthesis as a hypothesis. Do not invent extra examples, empirical support, citations, effect sizes, or a requirement that the user supply more domains before you can report the available result.

Name a framework only when a stable repeated structure benefits from a reusable label. Otherwise give the connection directly. Avoid abstraction whose only implication is "everything is connected."

## Deliver

Give the core synthesis, the source relationships supporting it, the exceptions or unresolved disagreement, and the decision implication or next discriminating test. Let important qualifiers survive compression. Ask for human input only when unavailable intent or domain knowledge would materially change the result and cannot be obtained from existing evidence.

Their examples generate hypotheses, not facts about the present case; their empirical claims require source verification.

**Find the relationship that survives the differences. Do not erase the differences to produce a relationship.**
