# Find the Unlock

Take another pass with greater ambition, not greater bulk. Find a lead worth developing together, not a performance of novelty.

## Locate the opening

Name the current approach, what it gets right, and the unresolved limitation worth investigating. Do not discard a good answer merely because it is familiar.

Identify the load-bearing assumption. Is it a real requirement, an observed limit, an untested belief, or an inherited workaround? Preserve the user's goal, chosen medium, and settled decisions. A missing measurement is not evidence that a constraint has disappeared.

Choose the move that could change the outcome:

| Obstruction | Useful move |
|---|---|
| The artifact is only a proxy for the desired outcome | Change the problem level without replacing the goal. |
| Summaries, approximations, or missing observations hide the mechanism | Change the evidence or instrument. |
| The current arrangement makes the obstacle unavoidable | Change the mechanism or a load-bearing premise. |
| Capability or cost assumptions may be obsolete | Check the actual capability and its operating boundary. |

Do not run every move. When no candidate is supplied, develop a plausible lead from the material already available; ask only for missing information that changes the investigation.

## Expose what changes

Describe the intervention as a causal chain:

**Specific change → dependency removed or capability gained → concrete experience made possible.**

Look for disproportionate leverage, but account for prerequisites, continuing costs, displaced labor, and guarantees. A proposal that saves the agent time by making the user supervise or recover more is not automatically an improvement.

Ground the chain in an example or artifact. If importing a mechanism, identify its operation and transfer boundary; inspect a supporting source when available, otherwise label it speculative. Do not invent demand, empirical effects, or citations.

Develop a rough candidate enough to expose its consequence before demanding a final plan. Then give it a serious adversary: an inconvenient case, competing explanation, or failure condition. Follow downstream implications only as far as the causal support permits.

## Advance rather than decorate

Use the smallest authorized investigation that could discriminate between the candidate and its strongest rival. That might be an inspected example, a contrasting workflow, a sketch, a safe prototype, or a measurement. A proposal to test is not an executed test; a promising thought experiment is not empirical validation.

Honor the requested effort and available access. A deeper pass should add evidence, causal detail, or a meaningful challenge—not invented experts, more adjectives, or mandatory new agents. If the necessary observation is unavailable, name it and keep the candidate provisional.

Return, compactly:

- The opening overlooked.
- The mechanism that could make it consequential.
- A concrete example of what becomes possible.
- The strongest reason it might fail.
- The smallest useful investigation or artifact to advance it.

These are the contents of the handoff, not compulsory headings for every conversational turn. If no genuine improvement emerges, say so; preserve what was learned about the constraint and the viable baseline.

Stop this pass when a specific lead has a mechanism, a failure boundary, and a next discriminating move—or when the available material cannot support a better lead. Do not declare the broader project finished or begin implementation without authority.

**Bring back the dependency we could change, not just the possibility we could admire.**
