# Thinking Systemically

Explain how the pattern reproduces itself, not merely how many things are connected. The useful model is the smallest one that changes where, when, or whether to intervene.

## Set the boundary around the outcome

Name the outcome, the behavior over time, and the decision at hand. Include a neighboring process only when it supplies a material input, absorbs a cost, or changes the feedback. Too narrow a boundary hides displaced costs; an unlimited boundary makes intervention impossible.

Start with observations rather than a named archetype. Separate what the data shows from the proposed explanation. The newest incident may be a symptom, not the system's governing condition.

## Distinguish what accumulates from what moves

Identify relevant **stocks** (backlog, inventory, unresolved obligations), **inflows**, and **outflows**, with compatible units and time horizons. A faster inflow can worsen the outcome when outflow remains constrained. A snapshot of a stock does not establish the rates that produced it.

Map only consequential causal links. For each, state the direction, operating condition, delay, and supporting evidence—or mark it as a hypothesis. A plus sign means variables move in the same direction under the stated conditions, not that the effect is good. A chain is not a feedback loop until a supported path returns to affect its starting variable.

Correlation alone establishes neither the arrows nor the loop. Check for a common cause, selection effects, or a changed environment. Do not diagnose people's health or motivation from a convenient causal diagram.

## Locate the binding dynamic

Ask which mechanism explains the observed recurrence:

- Does a local success increase demand or load elsewhere?
- Is a temporary fix weakening the capacity needed for a durable response?
- Does delayed information make intervention continue after enough has already been done?
- Does a locally rewarded metric diverge from the whole outcome?
- Does a finite resource or handoff constrain throughput, or a dependency path constrain delivery time?

These are candidate mechanisms, not guaranteed archetypes. State what observation would contradict the explanation. Do not call the most emotionally uncomfortable intervention the highest-leverage one.

## Intervene on a link and predict the response

For each serious intervention, identify the link, rate, delay, information path, or incentive it changes. Trace the immediate effect and the consequential feedback: what cost moves to another person, time, or system? Retain safety, access, and recovery constraints.

Prefer a bounded reversible test when authorized. State the expected directional response, the observation needed, and the relevant lag before interpreting it. Do not invent precision or repeatedly retune a system before the prior intervention can take effect. Monitor a possible displaced harm as well as the intended benefit.

Recommend the smallest supported intervention, no change, or a specific discriminating observation. The next step must be executable without mapping the entire world. A model that does not yet justify action is a hypothesis with a named gap—not a reason for endless analysis.

## Deliver

Show the minimal causal chain or loop, the evidence and uncertainty that matter, the proposed intervention, and what response would support or overturn it. Explain the time horizon and any material tradeoff. Use plain words; diagrams and framework names are optional.

Use [ARCHETYPES.md](references/ARCHETYPES.md) to generate hypotheses, never to diagnose a situation by resemblance. A simple known-good correction or urgent stabilization takes precedence over a full systems exercise.

**A useful system model predicts where the consequence goes—including the part outside the metric you improved.**
