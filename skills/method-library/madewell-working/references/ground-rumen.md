# Ground, Rumen, and successor preparation

## Ground: make the material inform the work

Reconstruct purpose, intended outcome and deliberate boundaries from actual supplied material. Inspect relevant existing artifacts before recommending replacement. State what each inspected source establishes and what it does not. Reading a file or storing its hash is not evidence of understanding; explain the finding that changes or supports the choice.

Keep a source identity distinct from its locator, and a claim identity distinct from its source. Preserve exact authorized material behind a summary. Attribute instructions to their actual author; distinguish explicit intent from interpretation and existing behavior from desired behavior. Unknown access is not absent functionality. A changed source is a new version, not replacement evidence under the old identity.

For each consequential finding, retain its source identity/locator, relevant version or observation time when known, finding and limitation. Inspect only material needed for the scope. A local path is not remotely accessible merely because someone cited it; a reference is usable only if the selected recipient can resolve it within their grant.

## Rumen: account for the applicable rules

Rumen carries rules through making and checking. Retain each supplied rule's identity, definition/version, applicability and evidence owner. Preserve the distinction between:

- **Fence:** a mechanical rule with a declared check, proved by the authorized mechanical executor. The maker carries the obligation; it does not award a pass or declare the fence inapplicable.
- **Judge:** a requirement needing independent judgment, owed by the assigned independent reviewer. A maker's explanation is input, not the review.
- **Sign:** operator-owned direction or taste guidance. Show how the work addresses it; do not manufacture an operator verdict.
- **Lexicon:** project vocabulary owned by its supplied definitions. Preserve meanings and flag unresolved conflicts instead of silently renaming concepts.

For each applicable rule, show how the contribution addresses it and what proof remains owed. Explain a genuine non-applicability using source and the authorized owner's determination where required. Missing rules, unavailable definitions and unavailable evidence remain explicit; an empty inventory is only valid when there are actually no selected rules, not when none were read. Record actual mechanical outcomes as passed, failed, not applicable or unavailable with executor and result version. Guidance does not become a shell command because it appears beside a fence.

Ground and Rumen span the appropriate functions; they are not additional agents, meetings, or stages. Discovery and any other configured consumer still need role-appropriate source/rule accounting.

## Prepare the recipient, not a generic summary

For every selected successor, provide purpose, allowed decisions/actions, expected contribution, the producer's actual result, resolvable permitted sources and versions, applicable rules/evidence owners, retained answers, unfinished questions, and dependencies. Check that material is available after environment preparation or restoration; stale producer material must be prepared again. Preserve the prior preparation as history.

Keep these different:

- A **cross-task preparation promise** says a producer task will deliver a prerequisite result before a consumer. Required ordering also needs a real dependency; prose does not schedule it.
- A **handoff** gives a specific recipient enough authorized material to do its job.
- **Environment setup** supplies tools, files or services. It establishes neither task completion nor semantic understanding.

Do not include private sibling output to make a handoff look complete. Do not invent a consumer for a standalone result. Blocked output retains partial material without claiming readiness. Save the contribution and preparation to the existing work record before the next exchange when that facility exists; otherwise disclose the persistence limitation.

**Example:** “Glossary G-2 inspected; ‘customer’ excludes trial users” is a grounded lexicon finding. A matching hash identifies the glossary bytes; neither proves the resulting audience list follows the definition. Its assigned checker still needs the list and the relevant glossary version.
