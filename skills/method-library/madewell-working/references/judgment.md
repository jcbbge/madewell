# Delegated judgment

Reconstruct context, the actual application purpose, intended outcome and deliberate commitments before judging a choice. Identify invariants and what is still unproved. Existing material is evidence, not scripture: understand what an existing choice supports before replacing it.

Apply the supplied rubric. The Made Well default gives equal standing to:

- **DX — developer experience:** understanding, change, inspection, diagnosis and recovery for those developing the work; not merely agent convenience.
- **UX — user experience:** usefulness, confidence, cognitive burden, recovery, craft and memorable/lovable experience for the person served.
- **AX — agentic experience:** discoverable correct action, inspectable state, verifiable results, bounded capabilities and reduced ambiguity/wasted context.

For each material alternative, name what each experience gets, costs and why; preserve uncertainty and longitudinal consequences. The default seeks improvement in at least two without averaging away a serious weakness in the third: three hits build with conviction, two proceed with the explicit sacrifice justified, fewer reshape or stop. “Toward 10×” is direction, not a measured multiplier or a reason to add features. Resource, latency, token, money and attention costs belong within the three experiences, not a fourth score.

When an actual matrix is supplied, retain its cell and verdict definitions. Do not invent cell numbers, weights or numeric precision for an absent matrix. A default rubric is a selected method, not immutable grammar or permission to override a task's hard constraints. Predictions remain predictions until actual observations support them.

Retrieve missing facts from authorized material. A delegated engineering choice stays with its owner. If materially plausible interpretations share a safe authorized next step, take it; do not force uncertainty to be resolved before it matters. A small authorized discriminating observation can settle a consequential unknown better than prolonged deliberation. Never fabricate an observation or present simulated agreement as independent evidence.

Keep the obvious option when evidence supports it. Prefer the least consequential downside and recurring burden that still achieves the intended outcome when the actual rubric leaves a tie. “Reversible” must include the real cost of discovering and recovering the mistake, not just undoing an edit.

Retain a proportional decision account: question, source/constraints, material alternatives, DX/UX/AX consequences and uncertainty, decision/owner, evidence that would change it, and proof/recovery implications. This may be a paragraph; no mandatory PRD, meeting or new agent follows.

Interrupt only for a reserved approval or missing consequential fact/preference that cannot be responsibly inspected, settled, deferred or contained. Give the exact missing decision, what it controls and a grounded recommendation. Delegated judgment cannot waive privacy, safety, fixed implementation scope, acceptance ownership or explicit approval boundaries. After the brief is fixed, return a defect through its question path rather than silently redesigning it.

**Example:** Reusing a documented export format may reduce maintenance and agent ambiguity while preserving the reader's familiar workflow. Those are reasoned DX/AX/UX consequences, not proof of a measured speedup; retain the actual compatibility observation and remaining usability uncertainty.
