The Problem

Four projects. Four walls.
One wall.

Made Well
Design law: mechanisms are properties of the pipe, never tools the agent may consult. But a contact point can only be structural if you own the pipe. In rented harnesses it degrades to prose and hooks — measured at 0% adoption across 45 runs for pull-based grounding.
Circadian
A memory metabolism — WAKE, SLEEP, REM — currently bolted onto Claude Code as SessionStart hooks. It works as a tenant. It was designed as an owner: phases of the loop, not decorations on it.
Strudel
The gateway over every harness's collection primitives — pantry searched by intent, code-mode composing tools in one flight. By its own doctrine "a superset, never a replacement" of Pi. Every decision Pi makes, it inherits — including the blocking ones.
Tower
Fleet orchestration — coordinator, orchestrator, workers, verbatim relay — impersonated by JSONL files, Stop-hook blocking, and polling. An actor system built from duct tape because the runtime offers no actors.

Each project is a multi-month derivation. Each terminates at the same theorem: the loop belongs to someone else.

The Standing

The convergence is not
under test. It already ran.

This is not an idea that needs validating. It is a conclusion that was reached four times, independently, by building — months of Made Well in live projects, Circadian through real wake cycles, Strudel against a real pantry, Tower across real fleets. Convergent derivation from independent starting points is the opposite of confabulation. It is how you know something is real.

And the industry is converging on the same point from the outside: everyone is trying to own the runtime harness, because the model stopped mattering. Everything here is framework-, provider-, and model-agnostic by construction. That is the direction of travel.

What remains open is exactly one question: the vehicle. The frame. The body everything latches onto. That is what the rest of this document decides.

The Spark

lisp-agent is a pamphlet,
not a skeleton.

138 lines. 3 commits. 112 stars. An HN front page. You will keep approximately zero of its code — what it demonstrates is that a complete agent is a recursive function over a message list.

;; the whole agent — base case: words. recursive case: run tools, recur. (defun agent-loop (messages) (let* ((message (ref (call-model messages) "choices" 0 "message")) (tool-calls (gethash "tool_calls" message))) (if (and tool-calls (plusp (length tool-calls))) (agent-loop (append messages (list message) (map 'list #'execute tool-calls))) (append messages (list message)))))

github.com/jamiebeach/lisp-agent · sync, single-threaded, eval-as-only-tool, explicitly a sandbox toy. The state is the argument being folded through the recursion — that insight survives even if Lisp doesn't.

The BEAM Question · Part I

Inside one SBCL image:
no. Structurally no.

You asked for BEAM-like composability at every layer. The two load-bearing BEAM guarantees cannot be replicated on shared-memory native threads. This is not an ecosystem gap; it is physics.

Heap isolation
Cannot. BEAM: per-process heaps, per-process GC. SBCL: one shared heap, stop-the-world GC — one thread's blowup is image-wide.
Safe preemption
Cannot. BEAM preempts every ~4,000 reductions. SBCL's terminate-thread is documented production-unsafe — a wedged thread cannot be safely killed.
OTP supervision
Not on the shelf. Sento (v3.4.3, May 2026, active) gives actor hierarchies and termination-watching — no restart strategies, no supervision trees, no restart-intensity semantics. And it can only restart actors that die, never actors that hang.
The BEAM Question · Part II

BEAM's guarantees live at the process boundary. Your architecture already has one.

Agents in this topology are OS processes — they must be. The Isolation Mandate demands it (the builder cannot verify itself), and workers are often other harnesses entirely. You do not need a million cheap green processes. You need a coordinator supervising dozens of workers.

At the OS-process boundary, everything BEAM promises is free: heap isolation (a crashed worker cannot corrupt the coordinator), preemption (kill -9 works even on a wedged worker — which in-image Sento cannot do), restart (respawn). uiop:launch-program provides spawn, monitor, kill, and exit codes, portably, today.

Erlang's processes were an isolation design that the BEAM later made cheap. You need the isolation, not the cheapness. The hand-built supervisor is on the order of a hundred lines — and it is exactly the shape Made Well already mandates.

The Kernel

The condition system is your escalation cascade as a language primitive.

Confirmed semantics: handlers bound by handler-bind run before the stack unwinds, and can invoke a restart that resumes execution at the signal point. Detection, policy, and recovery decouple across the stack.

;; worker: signals mid-computation, offers a way back in (restart-case (proceed-with (signal-decision-needed task)) (use-answer (answer) (continue-task task answer))) ;; coordinator: policy lives at the top, work never unwinds (handler-bind ((decision-needed (lambda (c) (invoke-restart 'use-answer (ask-human c))))) (run-worker task))

Read it against Tower: an agent hits a decision, signals, the coordinator answers, and the computation continues from exactly where it stood. No polling. No check_inbox. No state serialization. BEAM cannot do this — a crashed Erlang process restarts from scratch. TypeScript categorically cannot — exceptions unwind, always. This is the one asymmetric capability in the entire analysis.

The Second Advantage

The epistemics kernel
becomes native.

The EXPLORE note's design wants claims as typed values — (claim 1048576 :source "..." :fetched-at ts) — where an untagged claim is a type error, not a rule violation, and acquisition is a scheduler constraint the task graph enforces. That design wants a runtime where the structure of what is being asserted is inspectable data.

In Lisp, the agent's output, the runtime's plumbing, and code-mode scripts are the same substrate. Strudel's oven currently compiles to a QuickJS sandbox bolted onto Pi; here, the model writes forms into the image the pipe is made of, and the answer-weave pass walks them as data.

And the live image closes the loop: redefine the running harness mid-session. Circadian's REM pass as literal self-modification of a mind that never restarts.

Why Not the Other Four

“I'm okay with laying the pipe and the foundation. I just don't want to make the pipe. I don't want to make the lumber.”

Erlang, Gleam, TypeScript, Python — each survey ended the same way: minimal frameworks that demand you mold yourself to their shape, or raw materials that demand you mill your own. Common Lisp is the one candidate that is a lumberyard without a framework: the condition system, the live image, CLOS, macros, cl-completions, the MCP servers — dimensional lumber, milled, stacked, and unopinionated about your blueprint.

The loop stays yours. The materials come cut.

Costs · Unvarnished

LLM fluency: frontier models write CL measurably worse than TS — they miss unwind-protect idioms, occasionally miscount parens.

Bus factor: one person carries a disproportionate share of the modern CL LLM stack.

You: ten years of TS mastery stays home. Every idiom is new.

What offsets it

The REPL fix: the practitioner consensus cure for LLM-writes-CL is a live REPL wired in via MCP — which is, self-referentially, the architecture being built. Wire it in on day one.

The floor: cl-completions + ocicl + conformant MCP servers mean the provider layer, packaging, and wire protocol are not your problem.

The deploy story: a 12 MB binary that starts in 50 ms is a better ops surface than anything in the Node lineage.

The Road Not Taken

The two Lisps buy
opposite halves.

LFE — Lisp Flavored Erlang — is real and active (v2.2.0, January 2025, full OTP interop). Supervision trees, distribution, hot code reload: free, day one, battle-tested. But it is Erlang semantics in Lisp syntax — no CLOS, no condition system, immutable data. You would gain OTP and lose the resume-at-signal-point primitive and the live image.

If what you want is BEAM, LFE or plain Elixir is the honest choice. If what you want is the condition system, homoiconicity, and the image — SBCL with supervision at the process boundary is the choice. There is no third option that buys both. This verdict picks the second, because the escalation cascade is the beating heart of the topology, and only one of the two Lisps has it as a primitive.

First Cuts

Not a stress test.
A vehicle fitting.

The convergence ran for months; it does not need a probe. What the first build nights establish is fit and feel — whether this body latches onto the frame the way the theory says it does. Cut these boards first, in order:

01 · grounding
Claims as typed values, acquisition-before-assertion as a scheduler constraint in the loop. The EXPLORE note's design, made literal. This is the contact point that is "a cons cell away" — build it first because it is the thesis.
02 · escalate/resume
A worker signals a condition mid-computation; the coordinator invokes a restart; work resumes in place. Tower's ask_user without the polling. The asymmetric feature, demonstrated in the real loop.
03 · supervisor
Three worker processes via uiop:launch-program; kill one mid-task; one-for-one restart. ~100 lines. BEAM-at-the-boundary, proven on your machine.
day-one setup
ocicl, not Quicklisp. cl-completions as the provider layer. A CL REPL wired into the coding harness via MCP — the fix for LLM-writes-CL and a prototype of the runtime's own thesis, in one move.
